# Why Nimpol Chronicles (Preface)

Nimpol Chronicles did not begin as an AI project.

It began with one little boy.

William.

Like many neurodivergent children, William learns differently. Traditional school often focused on what he struggled with instead of building on his curiosity and strengths. Watching him become discouraged led me to start searching for another way.

One challenge stood out above the rest: spelling.

Because William loved fantasy and magic, I imagined spelling becoming literal magic. If he wanted to cast a spell in an adventure, he would have to spell the words correctly. Instead of practicing spelling for the sake of a worksheet, every correctly spelled word would have a purpose inside the story.

That simple idea became the spark.

At first I imagined creating an RPG with physical game cards. I even considered asking my adult daughter to help illustrate the cards. I began researching tabletop RPGs because I wasn't familiar with that world myself.

Then I told William about the idea.

Something unexpected happened.

He didn't just like the game.

He began creating an entire world.

He drew maps.

He invented Dragon Realm.

He described mountains, dragons, magical temples, disappearing landscapes, rivers filled with sea serpents, and a hidden treehouse where his wizard, Nimpol, lived with his mischievous squirrel companion, Nutty (who liked to bite monster's toes).

The project stopped being my idea.

It became ours.

I wanted someone—or something—to act as the Dungeon Master inside this world.

My first attempt was creating a Gemini Gem that could tell stories with William. While it was fun, it couldn't connect the story to real learning activities. It didn't remember enough about the world, couldn't use our own educational resources, and couldn't naturally encourage him to complete meaningful work.

So I built a prototype in Glide.

The app tracked experience points, attributes, and completed activities. The storytelling AI lived separately.

It worked...

but it never truly felt like one living adventure.

Then another idea changed everything.

I watched an interview about AI-supported personalized learning. One idea especially resonated with me: children often need far less structured instructional time than we assume when that time is focused on exactly what they are ready to learn.

I realized something.

I already had the beginning of that idea.

What if William's educational activities became meaningful because they moved his adventure forward?

What if the Dungeon Master knew which resources I had chosen as his parent?

What if the story adapted to his progress?

What if the world remembered?

At first I thought I was building a homeschool app.

Over time I realized I was trying to build something much bigger.

Not software that teaches children.

A world that grows with them.

Nimpol Chronicles is still inspired by one wizard named Nimpol.

One brave squirrel named Nutty.

One incredible world called Dragon Realm.

Those ideas will always remain at the heart of this project.

Everything else grew from there.
